parser: day-first dates read month first

This one covers 379 reports. The shared parser reads every date month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first, so a date like 7 April comes out as 4 July. It happens in the app, by email, and on the booking-v2 form, for all eleven tenants.

Fixed by fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales. Ticket closed as completed.

A few of these reports do not describe a clean swap: the desk read some as a one-day move (desk-notes.json), and some say only “the wrong day”. Their tenant and channel put them here, so they are counted here.

hint: booking-v3 tells people to type month first

This one covers 33 reports, all through the booking form of the three tenants on booking-v3. The form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People type what the hint says, and the form then reads the date the tenant’s way, day first. The parser is not in that path, so the parser fix leaves these reports as they are. Won’t fix: the booking-v3 form is retired, so the hint goes with it. Ticket closed as not planned. The parser fix alone would not have closed these reports.

What was wrong with the count

  • Merged on subject. Ten of the twelve subject lines in #4471 are shared by parser and hint reports. The subject cannot tell them apart; the tenant and the channel can.
  • Intake tags are a guess. intakeTag follows the reporter’s browser. Reporters on an en-US browser were tagged hint wherever they booked. Use the tenant and the channel, not the tag.
  • The export overlaps. The ticket system’s pages repeat some reports. Count each id once.

For the desk

  • Before a ticket is marked known, check that one fix closes every report in it. Here one problem closed with a fix and the other with a retired form.
  • Count by cause. A note on how a report reads is a footnote, not a new ticket.
